# Chapter 1

When the train stopped, I snuck off, into the woods, grabbing sticks as I climbed down into a small clearing. The trampled grass was now dead, which indicated that whoever had trambled it was long gone by now. I pulled some of it up and balled it together, pressing a hole in the middle. Then I set it down on the damp soil and piled sticks up around it, leaving enough room for the sparks from my flint and steel to catch and burn. And once the smoke rose up through the trees, I finally took out my bamboo sleeping pad from my bag and laid down on it. 

Before long, the amber glow cast my shadow on the trees around me, without creating very much smoke. I pulled out my map to see where I was, but I couldn't make sense of it. Had the train taken this line to the North? Or this one, to the East? I pulled out my compass to check the direction of the train line, but it was no use. I huffed and folded the map back up into my bag. 

When I heard some rustling come from the bush behind me, whipped out my rusty pocketknife and shouted, "who's there?"

"Easy!" A voice shouted back. "I just want to see if you have room around the fire!" 

"Stay away," I warned. "I've got a knife!"

"I'll give you some food, please!" A young, Feathered man came from between the trees, wearing torn pants and old hiking boots. He held a bag of oats out to me. "I've got water to boil it with, too."

His scarlet eyes shone against the glow from the fire in a genuine way, so against my better judgement, I put away the pocketknife and offered a space for him to sit down. He collapsed on the other side of the fire. 

"I'm Jay," he wheezed.

"Elira." I laid back down. "Where did you come from?" 

"Yakil," he explained. So the train had been going North, after all. "Went into the city to pick up more supplies. What about you?"

"Wolo," I answered. "Just one more stop on the train line."

"How long have you been train hopping," he asked. "I've been at it about four years now." 

I sighed. "About the same," I admitted. "Trying to get anywhere? Or just trying to survive?"

"Trying to survive," he answered.

"Same as you," I added. "Did you come from the other side of the Lake?" 

He nodded. "As you can probably imagine, I basically travelled straight here without stopping."

I snickered. "I've always wondered, do you have special skin treatments for all the feathers? Or do you use the same things that we Old Ones use?"

"As it turns out, when you're a chimera freak, the same old skin ointments work just the same," he chuckled. "Admittedly, there are all sorts of marketing schemes. _Your feathers deserve better. Try our new premium feather care product!_" He laughed. I laughed, too. "Well, you hungry?"

I shook my head. "Let the fire burn a little more. We want the coals to put the pot on."

So we waited and shot the shit a little more, and after about another hour, we put the pot on the remaining coals and filled it with water, and as soon as we did, three Horned ones appeared from out of the woods, shivering and covered in spikes. They were so young, and I felt so bad, so we let them join.

"Spreading our rations thin," I joked. "I'm Elira."

"Kamr," the taller girl responded. "This is Gico," she said, pointing to the middle sister, "and the youngest is Ama. Thank you for letting us join you for a bit."

"Yeah, no problem." They were also coming from Wolo, and had gotten chased away by the railway bulls. 

"We're refugees," they explained, "hopping trains just to try and make it somewhere we can stay for good."

"Refugees? Is there a war on the South side of the Lake or something?"

"There's always a war on the South side of the Lake," Jay joked, stirring in the oats to the boiling pot, but the girls nodded. 

I shrugged. "My bad, my bad."

So, we had oats, straight from the pot, an every man for themself type of supper. And when we were all fat and happy, we all leaned back, and I finally asked, "So, Jay, can you tell us your story."

He sighed. "Yeah, but only if you agree to be my travel partners."

"To where?"

"To Ura Aramal."

The four of us snickered. "You can't say you seriously believe in that place."

"Oh, I do, but first, let me tell you my story, so you can't leave without me."
