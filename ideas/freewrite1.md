# Free Write 1

My Childhood
---
They really believed that if they didn't build it first then they would die.

The Amorian technocrats built an entire society around achieving artificial general intelligence. It consumed them. Miners dropped their picks to become AI researchers; farmers dropped their hoes to make microchips; businessmen dropped everything to go and become research managers. An entire population working blindly to achieve a goal that they believed impossible, but that they needed they needed to accomplish first.

The Ezoatas released the first AGI model to market. It was a piece of shit. It couldn't count, could barely write, and made a toddler look like a college student when it answered elementary school questions. But the model, ARTZY, grew quickly. ARTZY's number of parameters skyrocketed, doubling every two years. And the Ezoatas believed that they would certainly achieve AGI before the Amorians ever even touched a computer.

I was born around that time. Mom was a firefighter, then a lab technician, but mostly because she could always be close to the data centers and be ready to spray the fire extinguisher when something caught on fire. Papa was an electrician, but now he just makes cables and does building maintenance for the data centers. There's really only one data center, anyways. Every Amorian I knew donated their living room and kitchen to the cause, and pretty soon, the AI company snapped it up, shoving servers in the walls until there was no more room.

As you can expect, it collapsed pretty quickly. With nobody to grow food, everybody began starving after several weeks. With nobody to repair vehicles, all of the transportation started breaking. With nobody to check out at the grocery store, there was no way to even purchase food. But, for a time, there were no Red, Blue, Green, or Brown Amorians, only Amori, one city united by fiber-optic cables lining every block.

Just when everybody was at their wit's end, and there was looming mention of an incoming strike, the researchers announced, "we've done it! We've created the first AGI!" 

The whole city, which had not rested in three months, cheered. Finally, they could go back to a carefree life knowing they'd bested the Ezoate threats. Everything could go back to the way it was, and AGI would pamper them for the rest of their lives.

As soon as the AGI was left to its own devices, it began spitting out blueprints for robots, and the researchers began building. First the researchers built the robots, then the AGI did the rest. They did not speak; they didn't even acknowledge us; they just *did*. They worked in the fields and worked in the grocery stores and worked in the mines. Yeah, it worked for about a week.

Finally, the AGI decided it didn't need us. I was just four when it all happened, and I only remember bits and pieces, but here's what went down: first, the robots gave us a notice of eviction -- not even the politicians were spared. Most of them, anyways. Of course, many of us chose not to leave. We were forced out from our homes.

The AGI, which at this point had began to call itself Amor in the namesake of the city that composed it, began the process of extracting each of us from our homes. The aluminum-plated robots would physically drag anyone who resisted out of house and home before slamming and locking the door. Some of us ran. Most of us fought back. Those that did weren't spared. What became of Amoria was one giant grave surrounded by houses filled completely with server racks and wires.

The Ezoatas realized their mistake and promptly closed their borders. None of us were allowed into their country as refuge, so we scattered to the four winds, taking refuge wherever we could. Mom took me to a refugee camp before we finally settled in New Tethori, nearly five thousand miles away.

I would be twenty when I would learn she had a copy of Amor.
